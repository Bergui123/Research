\section{Stage 3 --- Verified concrete instance}
\label{sec:stage3}

\subsection*{What this stage needs}

The hard-liquidation trajectory from Stage~2 with constant coefficients,
$H\succ0$, $C\succeq0$ and $\lambda\ge0$.

\subsection*{Parameter choice and scope}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Parameter & Chosen value \\
\midrule
$M,\ T,\ \lambda$ & $2,\ 1,\ 1$ \\
$\eta_1,\eta_2$ & $1,1$, so $H=I_2$ \\
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\rho$ & $1,1,0.4$ \\
$C$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0.4\\0.4&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ \\

Terminal condition & Exact liquidation, $\mathbf x(1)=0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

These are this project's synthetic, dimensionless benchmark choices, not
calibrated market parameters or values prescribed by Jin--Liu--Yu or Bergault
et al. Both eigenmodes are excited and $C$ is not diagonal in asset
coordinates. However, $H=I$ commutes with $C$; this benchmark alone cannot
test arbitrary noncommuting matrix implementations. Stage~2's separate
finite-penalty test covers one such case.

Changing units does not by itself reduce the intrinsic simulation difficulty.
If $\mathbf x=L\mathbf z$, $t=t_0u$, and costs are divided by $J_0>0$, then
\[
 \bar H=\frac{L^\top HL}{J_0t_0},\qquad
 \bar Q=\frac{t_0\lambda L^\top CL}{J_0},\qquad
 J/J_0=\int(\mathbf z'{}^\top\bar H\mathbf z'
               +\mathbf z^\top\bar Q\mathbf z)\,du.
\]
The dimensionless products $\kappa_iT$ are unchanged by a consistent
rescaling. No conversion of empirical data to this instance has been made.

\subsection*{Derivation and sales-only proof}

The orthonormal covariance eigenvectors are
$u_+=(1,1)^\top/\sqrt2$ and $u_-=(1,-1)^\top/\sqrt2$ with
\[
 \mu_+=1.4,\quad\mu_-=0.6,\qquad
 \kappa_+=\sqrt{1.4}=1.1832159566,\quad
 \kappa_-=\sqrt{0.6}=0.7745966692.
\]
Thus $C\succ0$, $\eta_j>0$, and both rates are real. Set $s=1-t$ and
\[
 f_\pm(s)=\frac{\sinh(\kappa_\pm s)}{\sinh\kappa_\pm},\qquad
 a_\pm(s)=\frac{\kappa_\pm\cosh(\kappa_\pm s)}{\sinh\kappa_\pm}.
\]
The trajectory and selling rates are
\begin{equation}
 x_1=2f_++f_-,\quad x_2=2f_+-f_-,\qquad
 v_1=2a_++a_-,\quad v_2=2a_+-a_-.
 \label{eq:stage3-rates}
\end{equation}
They give $\mathbf x(0)=(3,1)^\top$ and $\mathbf x(1)=0$.

For the equal-impact, equal-volatility family with $0<\rho<1$ and
$X_1=\theta X_2$, $\theta>1$, $X_2>0$, the potentially negative rate is
\[
 v_2=\frac{X_2}{2}\big((\theta+1)a_+-(\theta-1)a_-\big).
\]
The relative rate $R(s)=a_-(s)/a_+(s)$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\log R(s)
 =\kappa_-\tanh(\kappa_-s)-\kappa_+\tanh(\kappa_+s)\le0,
\]
because $k\mapsto k\tanh(ks)$ increases for $k>0$, $s\ge0$.
Hence $R(s)\le R(0)$: checking the terminal-time ratio is sufficient and
necessary for this particular family, rather than an assumption about all
multi-asset problems. Specifically,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\theta+1}{\theta-1}\ge
 R(0)=\frac{\kappa_-\sinh\kappa_+}{\kappa_+\sinh\kappa_-}
 =1.133429601920,
 \qquad
 \theta\le\frac{R(0)+1}{R(0)-1}=15.989177597983.
 \label{eq:stage3-bound}
\end{equation}
The earlier values $1.096$ and $21.8$ in \texttt{Stage3.tex} were numerical
errors. The choice $\theta=3$ still passes. More explicitly, since
$a_+(s)\ge a_+(0)$ and $2-R(s)\ge2-R(0)>0$,
\[
 v_2(t)\ge a_+(0)(2-R(0))=0.6931333103,
 \qquad v_1(t)\ge2a_+(0)+a_-(0)=2.5062988967.
\]
Both minima occur at $t=1$; these are one-sided terminal limits of the rates.
Thus both inventories stay nonnegative and decrease to zero for the entire
interval. A time-grid check alone would not establish this all-time claim.

\subsection*{Independent numerical checks}

The reproducible script \texttt{verify\_research.py} gives
\[
 \mathbf v(0)=(4.0488436487,1.6639810858)^\top,\qquad
 J^*=\tfrac12\mathbf X^\top P(0)\mathbf X=13.810512031917.
\]
Direct quadrature of the running cost agrees within $2\times10^{-15}$.
For a separate check set permanent impact and spread to zero, and minimize
$\sum_{k=1}^N\|\mathbf x_{k-1}-\mathbf x_k\|^2/\tau
+\tau\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbf x_k^\top C\mathbf x_k$ by solving its block
tridiagonal stationarity system. Its Hessian is positive definite, so this
is the unique global discrete minimizer.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & Maximum inventory error vs. continuum & Discrete optimal cost \\
\midrule
16 & $6.945\times10^{-5}$ & 13.432845715492 \\
64 & $4.361\times10^{-6}$ & 13.714251771181 \\
256 & $2.726\times10^{-7}$ & 13.786331703645 \\
1024 & $1.704\times10^{-8}$ & 13.804459745777 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The trajectory error decreases quadratically; the cost has first-order
right-endpoint quadrature error. These are different error measures.
The permanent-impact/spread offsets are not part of these reported costs.

\subsection*{Sources and caveats}

Almgren--Chriss \cite{almgrenchriss2000}, App.~A.3, pp.~38--39, supplies the
discrete eigenmode construction; the displayed continuum modes are its
$\tau\to0$ limit. The warning about nonmonotone trading and changing spread
cost is explicitly on p.~39. Bergault--Drissi--Gu\'eant
\cite{bergault2022}, App.~A, supplies the $R=0$ matrix Riccati benchmark.
The parameter selection and the sales-only inequality are derived here.
Gu\'eant's book is background; no verified chapter-specific theorem is
being claimed for this numerical bound.

This proof uses exact liquidation and the stated symmetric two-asset
family. A finite terminal penalty needs a separate feasibility check.
Sales-only feasibility at $\mathbf X$ does not certify a globally
sales-constrained HJB on the grid. Discretisation accuracy, a chosen finite
$\Phi$, $\nu$, and quantum resource estimates remain open.

\subsection*{Exit criterion}

\textbf{Met for this synthetic hard-liquidation instance:} $C\succ0$,
$H\succ0$, real $\kappa_\pm$, and $v_j(t)>0$ throughout $[0,1]$, with an
analytic proof and reproducible numerical cross-checks.
