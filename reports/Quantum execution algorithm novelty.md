# Prior work narrows the paper's novelty

Prior quantum work rules out broad claims such as the first quantum treatment of optimal execution, Hamilton--Jacobi gradient readout, or Schrödingerisation. The candidate contributions are narrower: a logical resource estimate for this continuous-time, HJB-based execution feedback pipeline and a grid theorem for a discrete heat equation with quadratic potential. The targeted searches found close work on quantum trading, Riccati/LQR algorithms, finite auxiliary intervals, and all-orders lattice expansions, but no exact match for the paper's specific grid hierarchy. The shifted recovery window is treated as prior art, not as a novelty claim. This search result does not establish priority. The report reflects primary-source searches through 2026-11-24 and does not audit the manuscript's mathematical or numerical correctness.

## Quantum trading and control already have precedents

Quantum annealing has been applied to discrete, multi-period optimal trading trajectories with transaction costs and market impact. This contradicts any unqualified claim that quantum optimal execution has not been studied, though the annealing paper does not present the manuscript's fault-tolerant logical-qubit and T-gate accounting ([Rosenberg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06182)). Quantum algorithms for matrix Riccati equations and the linear-quadratic regulator also predate this manuscript ([Krovi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07303)). A finite-horizon continuous-time LQR preprint from October 2026 returns control estimates and gives query and gate bounds, so the paper should not imply that its control output or finite-horizon scope is unprecedented ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26843)).

The finance literature also includes quantum and hybrid algorithms for financial PDEs and HJB models. Fault-tolerant resource estimates exist for derivative pricing, including detailed circuit estimates for autocallable and TARF benchmarks ([Chakrabarti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03819)). These papers address different algorithms and objectives. The narrow gap suggested by this search is a concrete logical-qubit and T-gate estimate for the manuscript's continuous-time, multi-asset execution feedback calculation using its HJB, compatible-diffusion Cole--Hopf, and Schrödingerisation route. Since this is an absence claim, it should be framed as the result of a documented search, not as a proven priority statement.

## Schrödingerisation and recovery ideas are established

The Schrödingerisation method, heat-equation recovery, and quantum Hamilton--Jacobi value and gradient readout all have prior work ([Jin, Liu, and Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14703); [Jin and Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07919)). A 2026 paper also combines Cole--Hopf with Schrödingerisation for regularized Wasserstein proximal operators, whose equations include a viscous Hamilton--Jacobi form ([Jin, Liu, and Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28752)).

Finite auxiliary-domain truncation, recovery regions set by spectral cutoffs, and smooth auxiliary profiles also predate this work ([Jin, Liu, and Ma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19123); [Jin and Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00370); [Nguyen and Tura](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02357)). The manuscript's half-integer Fourier convention may require its own phase and wrap derivation, but the general need for a finite-interval width bound is established. Likewise, shifting an imaginary-time generator by a scalar changes the evolution by an overall factor, a standard energy-shift operation ([Tang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17792)). The paper can claim a problem-specific recovery-window identity or implementation benefit if proved and compared directly with these results. It should not claim the general shift or finite-domain method as new.

## The grid expansion is the strongest candidate

All-orders modified-equation analysis is longstanding, and arbitrary-order lattice expansions with uniform remainders also predate the manuscript ([Warming and Hyett](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021999174900114); [Gurevich](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02673)). The narrower possible contribution is the explicit coefficient hierarchy for the manuscript's Gaussian solution under a time-varying quadratic potential, with a uniform lattice remainder and a recovered-rate correction whose quartic and quadratic terms have opposite signs. The targeted search found no identical result. That leaves a plausible, specific novelty claim, but it does not establish that no earlier paper contains it.

The distinction between the theorem and the costed result matters. The grid theorem may support a mathematical contribution, while the numerical box and profile constants used in resource selection remain sampled estimates in the current project record. The literature review does not validate the theorem, the Lean correspondence, the circuit costs, or the claimed finite-grid accuracy. Those checks remain separate submission blockers.

## State the contribution narrowly and keep searching

A supportable related-work statement would acknowledge prior quantum annealing for discrete optimal trading, quantum Riccati/LQR methods, quantum HJ gradient algorithms, and finite-interval Schrödingerisation. It could then state that the authors' focused search found no end-to-end logical-qubit and T-gate estimate for this particular continuous-time execution feedback pipeline. The grid result should be described by its exact operator, data class, norm, and remainder scope. The shifted-window result should be called a specialized recovery derivation, subject to the paper proving its discrete and finite-grid assumptions.

The search covered targeted arXiv and publisher-indexed queries through 2026-11-24. It was not a complete citation-chain review of conference proceedings, theses, patents, non-English work, unpublished results, or every bibliography of the closest papers. A scholar-led review of those sources is still needed before making a priority claim. The present result is therefore: **the broad novelty claims are unsupported; a narrower resource-accounting contribution and a special-case grid theorem remain plausible, not verified as novel.**
