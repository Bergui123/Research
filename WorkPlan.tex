\section*{Purpose and current position}

Estimate the logical qubits and T gates needed to solve an execution
problem that balances the cost of selling quickly against the price risk
of holding inventory. The route is
\[
 \text{execution HJB}\longrightarrow\text{Cole--Hopf linearisation}
 \longrightarrow\text{Schr\"odingerisation}\longrightarrow\text{resource count}.
\]
The two-asset execution problem has a classical Riccati solution, which
provides the reference answer. The contribution is a resource estimate;
no quantum speedup is claimed.

but remain provisional pending the independent review below.

\paragraph{Finite amplitude budget, revised.}
Let $L=(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{\rm grid})\|\mathbf v^*\|d/(2\sqrt2\nu)$
and $\delta=1-e^{-L}$. Suppose the three deterministic amplitude errors,
measured against the same reference amplitude, sum to at most $0.3\delta$,
and amplitude estimation has relative error at most $0.7\delta$ against
the prepared amplitude. Their composition lies between
$(1-0.3\delta)(1-0.7\delta)=1-\delta+0.21\delta^2$ and
$(1+0.3\delta)(1+0.7\delta)=1+\delta+0.21\delta^2$. The lower endpoint is
at least $1-\delta$. Multiplying the upper endpoint by $1-\delta$ gives
$1-0.79\delta^2-0.21\delta^3\le1$, so it is at most $1/(1-\delta)$.
Since $1-\delta=e^{-L}$, each node's log-amplitude error is at most $L$.
For each of the two rate components, the two unequal stencil-node errors add
in log space and are bounded by $2L$. The Euclidean rate error is therefore
at most $2\sqrt2\nu L/d$. The code now uses this finite allocation, computes
the estimation tolerance against the smallest allowed prepared amplitude, and
uses $\log(1+0.1\delta)$ for the potential-rounding share. Source: the
logarithmic recovery formula in Section~7 of the paper. Caveat: this is a
conditional error-composition statement; the numerical grid estimate,
individual deterministic-error bounds and circuit implementation remain to
be validated. Exit criterion: regenerate the counts and verify those
conditions for every selected configuration. After recalculation the 1\%
readout-A estimate is \QAOne{} logical qubits and $\TAOne$ T gates, compared
with $2.0\times10^{14}$ T before the finite-budget correction; the 0.1\%
estimate is $\TATenth$ T, compared with $2.6\times10^{17}$ T. The nine selected

\paragraph{Deterministic optimal-control verification.}
Let $V_c(\x,r)=\tfrac12\x^\top P_{T-r}\x$ with $P$ from the Riccati
equation, and let $J[\vv]$ be the finite-horizon cost for an $L^2$ control
and $\dot\x=-\vv$. The HJB identity gives
\[
 \vv^\top H\vv+\lambda\x^\top C\x+\frac{d}{dr}V_c(\x,r)
 =\left(\vv-\tfrac12H^{-1}P_{T-r}\x\right)^\top H
  \left(\vv-\tfrac12H^{-1}P_{T-r}\x\right).
\]
Integrating and using $V_c(\x,T)=\tfrac12\x^\top\Phi\x$ proves
$J[\vv]-V_c(\x,t)$ is the integral of this nonnegative square. The linear
feedback attains equality and has a finite-horizon solution. Source: direct
completion of the square applied to the HJB and terminal condition in
Step~2. Caveat: controls are unconstrained and square-integrable; this is a
paper proof, not a Lean formalization of the optimal-control statement.
The independent numerical check in
\path{review/validation/check_control_identity.py} tested 20 random
two-asset instances and sinusoidal controls; the largest absolute residual of
the integrated identity was $3.5\times10^{-10}$. Exit criterion: retain the
formalization caveat; the numerical test does not replace the proof.

\subsection*{Derivation and source}

For $\lambda C\succ0$, the coordinates $\mathbf y=U^\top H^{1/2}\mathbf x$
from Step 2 make the operator a sum of harmonic oscillators:
\begin{equation}
 \hat H=\sum_i\left[-\nu\partial_{y_i}^2+
 \frac{\kappa_i^2}{4\nu}y_i^2\right],\qquad
 E_{\mathbf n}=\sum_i\kappa_i(n_i+\tfrac12),\qquad
 \ell_i=\sqrt{2\nu/\kappa_i}.
 \label{eq:oscillator}
\end{equation}
These follow from the standard full-space oscillator spectrum, with
$\kappa_i=\sqrt{\lambda\mu_i}$. The levels are decay rates in
$e^{-s\hat H}$; the ground-state contribution decays as $e^{-E_0s}$,
$E_0=\tfrac12\sum_i\kappa_i$ ($E_0T=0.979$ for the benchmark; the grid
operators give $E_{\min}=0.975$--$0.978$).

For finite $\Phi$, the quadratic solution of Step 4 gives

\subsection*{Scale invariance (derived here)}

Put $\boldsymbol\xi=\mathbf x/\sqrt\nu$. Then
$\hat H=-\nabla_\xi\!\cdot(H^{-1}\nabla_\xi)+\tfrac\lambda4\boldsymbol\xi^\top C\boldsymbol\xi$
and $\psi(\mathbf x,0)=e^{-\boldsymbol\xi^\top\Phi\boldsymbol\xi/8}$, so
the rescaled problem contains no $\nu$:
\begin{equation}
 \psi_\nu(\mathbf x,s)=\psi_1(\mathbf x/\sqrt\nu,s),\qquad
 \mathbf v^*=-2\sqrt\nu\,H^{-1}\nabla_\xi\log\psi_1(\mathbf X/\sqrt\nu,T-t).
 \label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
Choosing $\nu$ therefore only moves the readout point
$\tilde{\mathbf X}=\mathbf X/\sqrt\nu$ relative to a fixed Gaussian.
Grid errors depend on $\Delta x/\sqrt\nu$, $L/\sqrt\nu$ and
$\tilde{\mathbf X}$. The norm ratio $\|\psi(T)\|^2/\|\psi(0)\|^2$ is
independent of $\nu$ ($0.134$ on every grid tested).

\section{Step 6: discretisation}
\label{sec:stage6}

\subsection*{Construction and bound}

Use $N_x=2^{n_x}$ points per asset on $[-L,L)$, with $\Delta x=2L/N_x$.
Each axis grid is shifted by less than $\Delta x$ so that $\mathbf X$ is a
node. For a central second difference,
\[
 (D_{2,a}\psi)_{\mathbf j}
 =\frac{\psi_{\mathbf j+\mathbf e_a}-2\psi_{\mathbf j}
 +\psi_{\mathbf j-\mathbf e_a}}{\Delta x^2}.
\]
Let $\mathsf X_a$ be the diagonal matrix of asset-$a$ grid coordinates. Then
\begin{equation}
 \hat H_d=-\nu\sum_a\eta_a^{-1}D_{2,a}
 +\frac{\lambda}{4\nu}\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\mathsf X_a\mathsf X_b,
 \label{eq:disc-operator}
\end{equation}
with the stencil/domain bound
\begin{equation}
 \|\hat H_d\|\lesssim\frac{4\nu}{\Delta x^2}\sum_a\eta_a^{-1}
 +\frac{\lambda L^2}{4\nu}\sum_{a,b}|C_{ab}|.
 \label{eq:norm-bound}
\end{equation}
The kinetic bound uses $\|-D_{2,a}\|\le4/\Delta x^2$; the potential bound
uses $|x_a|\le L$.

\subsection*{Choices made and checks (script \path{simulation/step5_nu_sweep.py})}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Boundary.} Periodic, i.e.\ $D_{2,a}$ built from cyclic shifts,
because that is what the circuit's incrementers implement. Dirichlet
boundaries change the feedback by at most $\BoundaryDiff$ at $N=64$ for all
seven $\nu$ (Step 5). $\hat H_d$ is real
symmetric and positive definite in both cases.
\item \emph{Spacing.} $\Delta x=4/k$, $k\in\mathbb N$, is the smallest
such value with $N\Delta x/2\ge L_\nu$. Then $\mathbf X/\Delta x$ and
all grid offsets are quarter-integers, so the circuit's coordinate
arithmetic is exact with two fractional bits (Step 8). This costs up to 19\% in $\Delta x$ at $N=32$ and at most 8\% for $N\ge64$.
\item \emph{Norm bound.} For $H=I$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm-bound} equals the
LCU normalisation $\alpha_H$ of Step 8, up to the comparator's power-of-two
scale. At $\nu=2$, $\lambda_{\max}(\hat H_d)=70.6,\,242,\,976$ against
$\alpha_H=90.8,\,261,\,995$ for $N=32,64,128$. The bound holds and is
nearly tight on fine grids, where the kinetic term dominates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Exit criterion (met)}

Hermiticity is preserved, the bound is compared with numerical
eigenvalues, and the recovered-control error against Riccati is quantified
as $\Delta x$, $L$ and $\nu$ vary (Steps 4 and 5). The chosen grids resolve
the Gaussian and contain the stencil around $\mathbf X$.

\section{Step 7: Schr\"odingerisation}
\label{sec:stage7}

\subsection*{Construction and source}

The heat evolution $e^{-s\hat H_d}$ is nonunitary. Following Jin, Liu and
Yu \cite{jin2023} (Sec.~3.1, Eqs.~3.2--3.4), set $w=e^{-p}\psi$ for $p>0$
and extend the initial data to $w(0,p)=g(p)\psi(0)$ on the real line,
where the source uses $g(p)=e^{-|p|}$. Fourier transforming in $p$ gives
\begin{equation}
  i\,\partial_s\hat w=H_S\hat w,\qquad H_S=-\hat H_d\otimes D_\eta,\qquad
  \|H_S\|=\|\hat H_d\|\,\eta_{\max}.
  \label{eq:schrodinger}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Mode picture and recovery (derived here)}

On an eigenpair $(E,\boldsymbol\phi_E)$ of $\hat H_d$ the transport equation
$\partial_sw=\hat H_d\partial_pw$ is a pure shift:
$w_E(s,p)=g(p+Es)\,\psi_E(0)$. If $g(p)=e^{-p}$ on $p\ge p_a$, then
$w_E(s,p)=e^{-Es}g(p)\psi_E(0)$ for all $p\ge p_a$, because $E\ge0$.
Hence $w(T,p)=e^{-p}\psi(T)$ on $p\ge p_a$. More generally, if $g=e^{-p}$ on
$p\ge0$ this holds for all $p\ge-E_{\min}T$, because every mode has
$p+ET\ge0$ there. The window may therefore start at $p_a=-cT$ with any
$c\le E_{\min}$. This is equivalent to shifting $\hat H_d$ by a constant and
needs no gates. Keeping $[p_a,p_a+\omega]$, $\omega=3$,
\[
 P_{\rm succ}=\frac{e^{2cT}(1-e^{-2\omega})}{2\|g\|^2}\,
 \frac{\|\psi(T)\|^2}{\|\psi(0)\|^2}.
\]
We use $c=0.98E_0$ and checked $c\le E_{\min}$ on every costed grid, including
all sweep points (\path{simulation/check_window.py}). On the discrete grid the
window starts at a grid point: before the revision of 24 November it started at the first
multiple of $\Delta p$ above $-cT$, and the realised gain was 3.5--5.6 instead of
$e^{2cT}$. The grid is now offset by $o=(p_a+L_p)\bmod\Delta p$, so that a point
sits at $p_a=-cT$; the circuit prepares $g(p_l+o)$ and nothing else changes. The realised gain is
\WindowGain{} ($g_1$, $\eta_{\max}=20$). Found by the independent review
(\path{review/claude-response-current.md}, finding 2).

\subsection*{Discrete $p$-grid: two findings}

The circuit uses $N_p=2^{n_p}$ points on $[-L_p,L_p)$ and the
half-integer frequencies $\eta_k=(k-\tfrac{N_p-1}2)\pi/L_p$, so
$\eta_{\max}\approx\pi/\Delta p$. On this grid the shift is spectral
interpolation of $g$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Wrap-around.} A mode with $ET+p_a+3>L_p$ wraps across the
periodic boundary and lands on the $e^{p}$ tail of $g$. Grid operators
have $E_{\max}\approx70$--$300$. The initial data carry relative spectral
weight from $3\times10^{-10}$ up to $10^{-5}$ above $E=40$, growing with
$N$ and $\nu$. A likely source is the nonzero boundary values of
$\psi(\cdot,0)$ along the weakly penalised $x_2$ direction; this was not
tested separately. With $L_p=16$ or $32$ and the smooth profiles the readout amplitudes at
$N=64$ are wrong by up to \UnsafeBench\% (benchmark), and by more than their own size
at $\lambda=4$. The rule
\begin{equation}
 L_p\ge\tfrac12\left(E_{\max}T+p_a+\omega+20\right),\qquad E_{\max}\le\alpha_H,
 \label{eq:safe-lp}
\end{equation}
bounds any wrapped contribution. The interpolant is antiperiodic, so the images of $g$ carry signs
$(-1)^r$; if $|g(p)|\le e^{-|p|}$ for $|p|\ge R$ and $|y|\le2L_p-R$, then
\[
 \Bigl|\sum_{r\ne0}(-1)^rg(y+2L_pr)\Bigr|\le\frac{2e^{-R}}{1-e^{-2L_p}}
 \qquad(\text{\lean{wrap_bound} in \lean{Wrap}}),
\]
because $|y\pm2L_pr|\ge R+2L_p(r-1)$ for $r\ge1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:safe-lp} gives the hypothesis with
$R=20$ for $y=p+ET$, $p$ in the window, $0\le E\le\alpha_H$; a wrapped contribution changes
$w(T,p,\x)$ by at most $2e^{-20}(1-e^{-2L_p})^{-1}\|\psi(0)\|$ (Cauchy--Schwarz over the eigenmodes).
Caveat: the lemma concerns the images of $g$; the interpolant differs from the antiperiodic extension by the
aliasing error, which is measured. The rule costs only $p$-qubits,
$n_p=\lceil\log_2(2L_p\eta_{\max}/\pi)\rceil$, because the walk cost
depends on $\eta_{\max}$ and not on $L_p$.
\item \emph{Smoothness of $g$.} The kink of $e^{-|p|}$ gives first-order
convergence in $\Delta p$. Smooth profiles that remove it are due to Jin, Liu,
Ma, Peng and Yu \cite{jlmpy}; we use a variant of their cut-off construction
(their Sec.~3.1),
\begin{equation}
 g_w(p)=\begin{cases}e^{-p},&p\ge0,\\
 S(p/w)e^{-p}+(1-S(p/w))e^{p},&-w<p<0,\\
 e^{p},&p\le-w,\end{cases}
 \label{eq:smooth-g}
\end{equation}
where $S(y)=f(y+1)/[f(y+1)+f(-y)]$ and $f(z)=e^{-1/z}$ for $z>0$,
$f(z)=0$ otherwise.
All derivatives of $S$ vanish at $y=-1,0$, so $g_w$ is smooth and its
Fourier coefficients decay faster than any power. Its bump on $(-w,0)$
lowers $P_{\rm succ}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Step 8: resource count}
\label{sec:stage8}

\subsection*{Circuit (derived here; script \path{simulation/step8_resources.py})}

\emph{Registers.} $x_1,x_2$ ($n$ qubits each), $p$ ($n_p$), PREPARE
ancillas $(t;k_1k_0;r_1..r_m)$, one GQSP signal qubit, arithmetic
workspace, and a phase-gradient register used by state preparation.

\emph{Block encoding of $\hat H_d-\alpha I$ ($H=I$).} The LCU is
\[
 \hat H_d-\alpha I\approx-\underbrace{\tfrac{\nu}{\Delta x^2}\sum_a(S_a+S_a^\dagger)}_{4\text{ equal terms}}
 -\tfrac{V'}2B,\qquad
 \langle0^m|H^{\otimes m}BH^{\otimes m}|0^m\rangle
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(1-\tfrac{2q(\mathbf x)}{2^m}\right),
\]
where $B=\sum_r|r\rangle\langle r|\otimes(-1)^{[r<q(\mathbf x)]}$ and
$q$ is the top $m$ bits of $5\mathbf x^\top C\mathbf x/\Delta x^2$
($2\rho=4/5$ gives integer weights $5,5,4$). Hence $V'q/2^m$ approximates
$V$ to within $V'2^{-m}$, and
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\frac{4\nu}{\Delta x^2}+\frac{V'}2,\qquad\alpha_H=2\alpha,\qquad
 V'=\frac{\lambda\Delta x^2}{4\nu}\,\frac{2^{k}}5,\quad
 2^k>\max 5\mathbf x^\top C\mathbf x/\Delta x^2.
 \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
The spectrum of $\hat H_d$ is in $[0,\alpha_H]$. The earlier circuit encoded $\hat H_d$ itself with normalisation
$\alpha_H$, i.e.\ it paid for an identity term. The constant $\alpha$ contributes $e^{i\eta\alpha T}$, a translation of
$p$ by $\alpha T$: the integer part (in units of $\Delta p$) relabels the window, the fractional part is added to the
offset of the prepared profile. This halves the walk length and removes two ancillas (independent review, finding 1).
PREPARE is one $R_y$ on $t$ (weights $4\nu/\Delta x^2:V'/2$) and Hadamards
elsewhere. SELECT applies $-S_a^{\pm1}$ as controlled $\pm1$ increments
and then flips the direction bit, which makes SELECT Hermitian.
The potential branch computes $z_a=j_a-o_a$ (exact: offsets are
quarter-integers), $|z_a|^2$, $|z_1z_2|$ with its sign, and $5q$ by five
additions. It then compares $r<q$, applies a phase, and uncomputes.

\emph{Circuit-level check.} For $n=2$ and $m=2$ (a
512-dimensional unitary) the explicit PREPARE and SELECT matrices give
$\mathrm{SELECT}^2=I$ and
$\langle0|\mathrm{PREP}^\dagger\mathrm{SELECT}\,\mathrm{PREP}|0\rangle
=(K+V_m-\alpha I)/\alpha$ exactly, with $|V_m-V|\le V'2^{-m}$. The walk
operator's eigenphases contain $\pm\arccos[(E-\alpha)/\alpha]$ for every
eigenvalue $E$.

\emph{Gate-level compilation of SELECT} (\path{simulation/compile_select.py}). SELECT is a permutation of basis states
with signs. The script builds it from $X$, CNOT, AND, AND$^\dagger$ (measurement based, no T), $Z$ and $CZ$: controlled
increments; coordinates by one constant addition; absolute values; two squarers (off-diagonal products only, carries
limited to three bits beyond the row, checked on all inputs up to 11 bits); one multiplier; in-place accumulation;
a carry chain for $[r<q]$; $CZ$; uncomputation. The gate list is simulated exactly on every grid point and on
$2\times10^5$ random basis states of each of the \CompileCount{} selected configurations (benchmark and sweeps, A and
B). It returns the correct state, sign and $q$, and all work qubits to zero. The resource script charges this
circuit (\path{simulation/select_circuit.py}). Qualtran's block costs for the same layout, charged before, differ from it by
between $-\CompileMax\%$ and $+\CompileMin\%$: at $\rho=0$ they undercount, which an assertion in the sweeps exposed. Caveat: only
SELECT is compiled; reflections are multi-controlled $Z$ with the standard count, and PREPARE is one rotation.

\emph{Time evolution.} With $u=\sum_jb_j2^j$ and
$\eta=\Delta\eta(u-\tfrac{N_p-1}2)=\sum_j s_j2^{j-1}\Delta\eta$,
$s_j=\pm1$, the evolution factorises:
$e^{-iH_ST}=\prod_j e^{is_j\tau_j\hat H_d}$ with $\tau_j=2^{j-1}\Delta\eta T$
and $\sum_j\tau_j=\eta_{\max}T$. The sign $s_j$ costs a Clifford $Z$ on bit
$j$ at every SELECT. Each factor is a GQSP polynomial in the qubitization
walk \cite{motlaghwiebe2024,berry2024gqsp}. It uses $K_j+3$ walk calls ($K_j+1$ directionally controlled, two
controlled, \cite{berry2024gqsp} Thm.~2 and Eqs.~(9)--(11)), where $K_j\ge\alpha\tau_j$ is even. Standard QSP needs about
$2K_j$ calls.

\emph{Tail budget.} Let $\theta_j=2\sum_{k>K_j}|J_k(\alpha\tau_j)|$. The truncated pair $(P_K,Q_K)$ is
rescaled by $1/(1+\theta_j)$ and completed by $(P',Q')$ with $P'^2+Q'^2\le4\theta_j$ to leading order. In the block
$\langle+|\cdot|+\rangle$ the completion cancels, so the block errs by at most $a_j=2\theta_j$. Unitarity bounds the leak
to $|-\rangle$, and the return from it, by $\sqrt{2a_j}$. After $n_p$ segments the success branch errs by at most
$\sum_ja_j+\tfrac12(\sum_j\sqrt{2a_j})^2$ to leading order, i.e.\ $2n_p(n_p+1)\theta$ for equal tails. We therefore require
$\theta_j\le\varepsilon_{\rm state}/[4n_p(n_p+1)]$; the other half of $\varepsilon_{\rm state}$ is for rotations. Source:
derived here. Caveat: not formalised; the rotation and tail errors are added at first order.

\emph{Gate costs.} SELECT is charged at the AND count of the compiled circuit
(Toffoli and logical-AND both 4 T \cite{gidney2018}); multi-controlled $Z$ from Qualtran 0.7 \cite{qualtran}.
Compute and uncompute are both counted. Rotations cost
$3\log_2(1/\varepsilon_{\rm rot})+2$ T \cite{rossselinger2016}, four per
walk step (PREPARE, PREPARE$^\dagger$, GQSP). Gaussian state preparation
uses QROM-driven Grover--Rudolph \cite{groverrudolph2002,babbush2018}:
about $2\cdot2^{2n}+2n\,b_{\rm rot}$ Toffolis for $\psi(0)$ and likewise
for $g(p)$. Each QFT on $p$ is costed at $n_p^2$ Toffolis.

\emph{Error budget.} Let $\varepsilon$ be the target relative error of
$\mathbf v^*(\mathbf X,0)$. The grid error $\varepsilon_{\rm grid}$
(Steps 5, 9) must be $\le\varepsilon/2$. The remainder is converted into a
relative-amplitude budget $\delta=1-e^{-L}$, where
$L=(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{\rm grid})\|\mathbf v^*\|h\Delta x/(2\sqrt2\nu)$.
It is split 70\% to amplitude estimation and 10\% each to
Schr\"odingerisation, coherent simulation (GQSP truncation plus
rotation synthesis, $\varepsilon_{\rm state}=0.1\,\delta\,b_{\min}$) and
potential truncation ($m=\lceil\log_2(TV'/\log(1+0.1\delta))\rceil$,
subject to the exact-arithmetic cap).

\emph{Potential rounding.} $-\hat H_d$ has nonnegative off-diagonal entries, so
$e^{-s\hat H_d}$ preserves positivity; if $0\le V-V_m\le\delta_V$ then
$\psi\le\psi_m\le e^{T\delta_V}\psi$ entrywise. This replaces the earlier
heuristic estimate.

\emph{Qubits.} At the peak of the potential oracle,
$N_q=2n+n_p+(3+m)+1+N_{\rm work}+b_{\rm rot}$ with $N_{\rm work}$ the peak workspace of the compiled SELECT, plus one
recycled control qubit for amplitude estimation, with $b=n+3$ bits per
coordinate (readout A) or $n+1$ (readout B, centred grid).

\subsection*{The direct polynomial (what the auxiliary variable costs)}

$\hat H_d$ is Hermitian and nonnegative, so $e^{-T\hat H_d}$ needs no auxiliary variable. With
$x=\hat H_d/\alpha-1\in[-1,1]$ and $\beta=\alpha T$,
\[
 e^{-T\hat H_d}=e^{-\beta}\Bigl[I_0(\beta)+2\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^kI_k(\beta)T_k(x)\Bigr],\qquad

window factor. Script \path{simulation/direct_polynomial.py}: same grids, budgets and arithmetic as the selected

\section{Step 9: readout}
\label{sec:stage9}

\subsection*{Readout A: pointwise amplitude estimation}

\begin{equation}
 v_a=-2\nu\,\eta_a^{-1}\,
 \frac{\log b(\mathbf X+h\Delta x\mathbf e_a)-\log b(\mathbf X-h\Delta x\mathbf e_a)}{2h\Delta x},
 \qquad b_j=\sqrt{P_{\rm succ}\,p_j}.
 \label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
$b_j^2$ is the probability of the joint event ``$p$ in window and
$\mathbf x=\mathbf x_j$'', so $P_{\rm succ}$ cancels in the ratio. Relative
errors $\delta_j<1$ give
$|\Delta v_a|\le(\nu/(h\Delta x))\sum_\pm[-\log(1-\delta_{a\pm})]$.
Amplitude estimation \cite{brassard2002} with $M_j=2^{t_j}$ phase outcomes has relative
error at most $\pi/(M_jb_j)+\pi^2/(2M_j^2b_j^2)$ with probability at least
$8/\pi^2$; we charge $2M_j+1$ coherent runs, a conservative allowance.
A semiclassical Fourier transform
\cite{griffiths} needs one extra qubit. Each $t_j$ is chosen to meet its
allocated per-node budget. The median of $r=11$
repetitions gives 95\% joint confidence (binomial bound). Iterative variants
\cite{grinko2021} may be cheaper.

\subsection*{Readout B: sampling (quadratic models only)}

$|\psi(\mathbf x,T)|^2\propto e^{-\mathbf x^\top P_T\mathbf x/(4\nu)}$ has covariance
$\Sigma=2\nu P_T^{-1}$. Accepted samples of $\mathbf x$ give
$\hat P=2\nu\hat\Sigma^{-1}$ and the rate $\tfrac12\hat P\mathbf x$ for every
$\mathbf x$; by scale invariance $\nu=1$ suffices and $\mathbf X$ need not be a
node. Error metric: $\|(\hat P-P_T)P_T^{-1}\|_2$, uniform in $\mathbf x$. The
systematic part $r$ adds grid error and rounding plus Schr\"odingerisation
error in norm, both measured against the known $P_T$.

\emph{Coherent error.} If the accepted vector $v$ ($\|v\|^2=P_{\rm succ}$, support in a box of half-width $L$, so
$|\x|^2\le2L^2$) is replaced by $v'$ with $\|v'-v\|\le e$, the unnormalised second moments change by at most
$2L^2(2e\sqrt{P_{\rm succ}}+e^2)$ and the normaliser by $2e\sqrt{P_{\rm succ}}+e^2$; the normalised matrix changes by at most
$8L^2e/\sqrt{P_{\rm succ}}$ to first order, and the metric by that over $\sigma_{\min}(\Sigma)$. The script takes
$e=0.01\,\varepsilon P_{\rm succ}\sigma_{\min}/(8L^2)$ and charges $0.02\,\varepsilon$.

\emph{Samples and runs.} The 96\% quantile of the statistical part is $\kappa/\sqrt{n_s}$, $\kappa=\KappaB$ (large-sample Wishart limit, Monte
Carlo), so $n_s=[\kappa/(0.98\,\varepsilon-r)]^2$. The number of runs is the smallest $R$ with
$\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(R,P_{\rm succ})\ge n_s]\ge0.99$; $0.96\times0.99>0.95$. Script
\path{simulation/step9_sampling.py}. The first factor is asymptotic; the emulation tests it with a one-sided
Clopper--Pearson bound at level 0.001. This readout uses the Gaussian form of
$\psi$ and does not extend to nonquadratic costs.

\subsection*{Exit criterion (met, with the stated scope)}

The acceptance fluctuation and the coherent error are charged; the emulated success probabilities have lower bounds of at least
\LowerBound\%. Synthesis errors are budgeted and not emulated; the Wishart quantile is asymptotic.

\subsection*{Non-quadratic data}

\emph{Barrier.} With $\psi_i=e^{-W_i/4\nu}$ on a grid, keeping one term of the normalisation gives
\[
 p_j=\frac{\psi_j^2}{\sum_i\psi_i^2}\le e^{-(W_j-W_k)/2\nu}\quad\text{for all }j,k
 \qquad(\text{\lean{amplitude_barrier}}).
\]
If the recovered control has a relative bias of at least $\beta\nu$ that must not exceed $\varepsilon$, then
$p_j\le e^{-w\beta/2\varepsilon}$ with $w\le W_j-W_k$ (\lean{barrier_of_bias}). All benchmark optima use $\nu\ge4$, which is
allowed only because the quadratic feedback does not depend on $\nu$.

\emph{Recentring.} Let $\boldsymbol\xi'=-2\nu H^{-1}\boldsymbol\alpha$, $\boldsymbol\alpha'=-G(\boldsymbol\xi)$,
$\gamma'=\nu\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot H^{-1}\boldsymbol\alpha+G(\boldsymbol\xi)\cdot\boldsymbol\xi-V(\boldsymbol\xi)$ and
$\psi=e^{\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\x+\gamma}\phi(\x-\boldsymbol\xi,s)$. Then
\[
 \psi_s=\nu\nabla\!\cdot(H^{-1}\nabla\psi)-V\psi\quad\text{if}\quad
 \phi_s=\nu\nabla\!\cdot(H^{-1}\nabla\phi)-[V(\boldsymbol\xi+\mathbf y)-V(\boldsymbol\xi)-G(\boldsymbol\xi)\cdot\mathbf y]\phi .
\]
Proof: substitute; the terms $2\nu\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot H^{-1}\nabla\phi$ and $\boldsymbol\xi'\cdot\nabla\phi$ cancel, and the remaining
coefficient of $\phi$ is $\nu\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot H^{-1}\boldsymbol\alpha-\boldsymbol\alpha'\cdot\x-\gamma'-[\dots]=-V(\x)$.
(\lean{recentre}, \lean{recentred_feedback}; the derivatives are hypotheses.) With $G=\nabla V$ the curves are the extremals of the control
problem in time remaining, $\boldsymbol\pi=-4\nu\boldsymbol\alpha$ is the costate, and the feedback is the reference control plus
$-2\nu H^{-1}\nabla\log\phi$. For quadratic running cost the operator and the circuit do not change.

\paragraph{Direct polynomial.}
For a walk eigenvalue $z=e^{i\theta}$, $x=(z+z^{-1})/2$ and a degree-$K$ Chebyshev polynomial in $x$ has Laurent degree $K$.
Multiplying by $z^K$ gives degree $2K$, hence the $2K$ GQSP signal-query count. Let
\[
 \tau_K=2e^{-\beta}\sum_{k>K}I_k(\beta),\qquad
 p_K(x)=e^{-\beta}\Bigl[I_0(\beta)+2\sum_{k=1}^{K}(-1)^kI_k(\beta)T_k(x)\Bigr].
\]
For $x\in[-1,1]$, the uniform Bessel-tail estimate gives
$\|p_K-e^{-\beta(1+x)}\|\le\tau_K$ and $\|p_K\|\le1+\tau_K$. The contraction assumed for GQSP is
$p_K/(1+\tau_K)$, for which
\[
 \left\|\frac{p_K}{1+\tau_K}-e^{-\beta(1+x)}\right\|
 \le\frac{\tau_K+\tau_K\|e^{-\beta(1+x)}\|}{1+\tau_K}\le2\tau_K.
\]
The resource script allocates $\tau_K\le\varepsilon_{\rm state}/4$, so this term is at most
$\varepsilon_{\rm state}/2$. The analytic tail argument is uniform; the numerical check in
\path{simulation/direct_polynomial.py} applies the scaled series on finite tested grids and is corroboration, not a uniform proof.
The script now uses that same scaled output to compute point amplitudes and $P_{\rm succ}$ for the direct-polynomial resource estimates.
The regression test \path{simulation/tests/test_direct_polynomial.py} first failed on the old behavior: for its hand-checkable
$\beta=1$, $K=1$ case it returned amplitude $1$ instead of the contraction-scaled value $0.7882376779493919$. After the correction,
\texttt{PYTHONPATH=. ../.venv/bin/python -m unittest tests.test\_direct\_polynomial} passes. The production check also verifies the
finite-grid error against both $2\tau_K$ and $\varepsilon_{\rm state}/2$.
The $2K$ query structure is supported by Motlagh and Wiebe, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01501v2}.

\paragraph{Jin--Liu comparison.}
In v2 of Jin and Liu, \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07919v2}, their Lemma 12 statement omits a point-overlap term that its proof derives:
\[
 J=\frac{(1/q+1/\|\mathbf u\|^2)^2}{\|\mathbf u\|^2},\qquad
 q=|\langle x_a|u\rangle|^2.
\]
For unit-norm input and contractive evolution, $\|\mathbf u\|^2\le1$, hence $J\ge q^{-2}$.
The paper now attributes this factor to the proof, not to the lemma statement, and distinguishes their absolute HJ-value error from
the relative control error here. This comparison does not establish an impossibility for other access models.
